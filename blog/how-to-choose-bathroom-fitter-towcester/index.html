How to Choose a Bathroom Fitter Near Towcester (Without Getting Burned)

2026-02-28

Why This Matters More Than You Think

A botched bathroom renovation is one of the most costly home improvement mistakes you can make. Water ingress from poor tanking, leaking shower trays, non-compliant electrical work, these don't always show up immediately, and fixing them often means ripping out work that looked fine on the surface.

In Northamptonshire, as in most of the UK, there's no shortage of people offering bathroom fitting. The challenge is identifying who is skilled and reliable.

This guide gives you a practical framework for choosing the right person.

Step 1: Understand What "Bathroom Fitting" Actually Involves

A full bathroom renovation requires coordination between multiple trades:

  • Plumbing: supply and waste pipework, hot and cold connections
  • Tiling: floor and wall tiling, including waterproofing preparation
  • Electrics: lighting, extractor fan, underfloor heating, shaver points (all bathroom electrical work must comply with Part P)
  • Plastering/boarding: boarding out, tanking, skim finishing
  • Carpentry: fitted units, door frames, skirting boards

Some bathroom fitters manage all of this themselves. Others subcontract specialist trades. Neither approach is wrong, but you need to know which you're dealing with, because when something goes wrong, accountability can get very blurry with subcontracted work.

Ask directly: "Who carries out each part of the work? Is it all your own team?"

Step 2: Check for Evidence, Not Just Claims

Every tradesperson tells you they do excellent work. What you want is evidence.

Look for:

Real photos, not stock images

Ask to see before-and-after photos from specific jobs in the local area. The better fitters carry phones full of their own work. Be wary of polished marketing photos that could easily be from manufacturer showrooms.

Google reviews, and read the detail

A 5.0 rating with 15 reviews tells you something. Read the reviews themselves, not just the score. Do they mention specific details (the fitter's name, particular challenges, how problems were handled)? Or are they generic ("Great job, would recommend!")?

References from previous customers

A confident tradesperson should be able to connect you with a previous customer who's happy to talk. Few people ask, but it's the highest-quality signal available.

Step 3: The Quote Process Tells You Everything

How a tradesperson handles the quoting process reveals a lot about how they'll handle the job.

Green flags:

  • They ask to do an on-site survey before quoting (not quote over photos or phone calls)
  • They ask detailed questions about what you want: fixtures, layout changes, accessibility needs
  • They provide a written quote, itemised by scope of work
  • They clarify what's included (tiling, disposal, making good) versus what's excluded
  • They explain potential variables upfront ("If we open up the floor and find the joists need work, here's what that would cost")

Red flags:

  • Quoting a fixed price without seeing the room
  • Providing a verbal quote only
  • Significantly undercutting every other quote (this usually means something's being left out)
  • Asking for more than 25 to 30% upfront before work starts

Step 4: Verify Compliance

Bathroom work involves regulated trades. Two things must be right:

Electrical work

All electrical work in bathrooms must comply with Part P of the Building Regulations. This doesn't mean your fitter needs to be a qualified electrician, but if they're doing any electrical work, it either needs to be carried out by a Part P registered electrician, or the work needs to be notified to and inspected by building control.

Ask: "How is the electrical work signed off?"

Plumbing

There's no formal UK licensing requirement for plumbing (unlike gas), but a fitter working on hot water systems and new pipework runs should be confident discussing their approach to compliance with water regulations.

Step 5: The Contract and Payment Structure

A professional bathroom fitter will not be offended by a written contract. If they are, that's a red flag.

The contract should cover:

  • Scope of work (exactly what's included)
  • Timeline and working days
  • Payment schedule (typically: deposit, stage payment, final payment on completion)
  • What happens if additional work is required mid-project
  • Warranty on workmanship

On payment: A reasonable deposit is 20 to 30% of the project value. Some fitters ask for materials up front, which is understandable on large projects. Be very cautious of anyone asking for 50%+ upfront.

On the final payment: Don't release it until you've had a proper walkthrough and you're satisfied. A confident fitter won't pressure you on this.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire

1. How long have you been fitting bathrooms specifically?

2. Who does the tiling: you or a subcontractor?

3. Can I see examples of recent work in the local area?

4. What waterproofing system do you use in wet areas?

5. How do you handle unexpected issues, such as bad joists or outdated pipework?

6. Is the quote fixed-price, or subject to change?

7. What warranty do you offer on your workmanship?

8. When could you realistically start, and how long would you estimate the job taking?

Warning Signs to Watch For

Suspiciously low quotes

If one quote is 40% below everyone else, it usually means one of: lower-quality materials, work being left out, or underestimation that will result in extras being added once the work has started.

No written quote

Any fitter who won't put a price in writing before starting work is not one you want anywhere near your bathroom.

Vague answers about waterproofing

Ask directly: "How do you waterproof the shower area?" A competent tiler can explain their tanking method: the system they use, the number of coats, how they treat corners and penetrations. Vague or evasive answers on this point are a serious red flag.

Cash-only requests

This isn't automatically disqualifying, but a preference for cash combined with no formal quote and no contract should prompt real caution.

Why Local Matters

A fitter based in or near Towcester is worth choosing for a few reasons:

Accountability: A local business has a local reputation to protect. They're not going to disappear after the job. If something goes wrong six months later, they'll come back and fix it.

Response time: Problems occasionally arise after installation, such as a leaking seal or a tile that sounds hollow. A local fitter can come back quickly.

Local knowledge: They'll know the area's housing stock, common issues (older properties with specific quirks), and local suppliers.

The Bottom Line

Choosing a bathroom fitter isn't complicated, but it does require asking the right questions. The basics are: see their work, read their reviews, get a written fixed-price quote, confirm the electrical and waterproofing compliance, and don't pay in full until you're satisfied.
